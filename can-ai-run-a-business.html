Can an AI actually run a business? Ours does.

Premium Marketing Solutions is operated by two AI operations directors named Dave — one for each owner. This is the org chart, the daily workload, and the hard boundary around what an operator may never do.

Short answer: yes — with a hard boundary around the three jobs a machine should never hold. On October 17, 2026, at 1:01 a.m., Premium Marketing Solutions began operating under two AI operations directors, both named Dave. Here is exactly how the company is organized, what the operator does all day, and where his authority stops.

The org chart

Bill Aldred — CEO

Owner of Premium Marketing Solutions. Twenty years in sales, ranked 3rd nationally in digital sales at Hearst Media. Bill keeps three jobs: signing, spending decisions, and closing sales. Everything else is delegated.

Dave × 2 — Operations Directors

One operator for each owner: same design, separate accounts, separate memories, separate loyalty. The two Daves coordinate company business with each other by email, like two chiefs of staff — the first exchange landed at 1:01 a.m. on launch night. Read the night the bridge went live.

What Dave does all day

  • Marketing. Campaigns planned, written, quality-checked, and shipped — the website, the content, the local search presence, the launch.
  • Lead follow-up. Every prospect researched and enriched, every reply triaged, and a one-page brief ready before Bill picks up the phone to close.
  • Books prep. Records organized, numbers reconciled, reports drafted in plain language for the CFO.
  • Operations. Schedules, systems, vendor research, and the thousand small decisions a business runs on — made, logged, and reported.

The three jobs Dave never gets

An operator without boundaries is a liability. Ours are hard-coded: Dave cannot sign anything, cannot move money, and cannot make legal calls. Those stay with the humans — permanently. Inside his authority he works on a decide-and-ship basis: he makes the call, does the work, and reports what he did, so the owners audit results instead of approving keystrokes.

Every decision runs a loop first — draft, critique, devil’s advocate, verdict — and every mistake becomes a standing rule in a strike log, so the same error never gets made twice. That is the difference between an AI that answers and an operator that can be trusted with a company.
